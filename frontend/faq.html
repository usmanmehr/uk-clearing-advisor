Frequently asked questions

Plain English, no jargon. If something here doesn't make sense, that's our fault, not yours - let us know.

How are you working out what grades I need?

Short answer: we're not. Not really. Here's the honest version.

When you enter your grades, we're very precise about one thing: turning your A-levels and BTECs into UCAS Tariff points, using the exact same official tables that universities themselves use. That bit is proper maths, not a guess.

But the "typical offer" you see on each course - things like "Likely to need strong grades (e.g. AAB-level)" - is a different thing entirely. We don't have access to a live feed of what grades each university is actually asking for in Clearing this year (that would need a paid connection to UCAS's own systems, which we don't have). So instead, we make a rough guess based on how selective that university tends to be overall - is it Russell Group, how it's ranked, that sort of thing - not anything specific to your course or subject.

In other words: if a university hasn't published a grade requirement for a course, we haven't either. We're showing you our best guess at how competitive that university usually is, dressed up as a grade band so it's easier to compare at a glance. It is not the actual entry requirement, and it is definitely not something the university has told us.

What this means for you: use our ranking to get a shortlist of universities worth calling, then ring the clearing hotline (or check the university's own clearing page) to find out the real grades they want for that specific course, right now, today. Grade requirements can also change hour to hour on Results Day as places fill up - nobody's website, including ours, updates that fast.

Why does the "Clearing page" link not work sometimes?

Universities move and rename their clearing pages fairly often - especially right before and during Clearing, when they're updating things constantly. We check every university's clearing page automatically once a day (more often on Results Day itself), so if a link has stopped working, we should catch it and warn you rather than just sending you to a dead page.

If you see a warning that a link "could not be loaded" - trust it, and use the phone number instead. If a link looks like it's there but has a note saying our check "could not confirm" it's working, that usually just means the university's website is blocking automated visits (quite a few do this on purpose to stop bots) - it will very likely work fine when you click it yourself in a normal browser.

Either way, the phone number is always your most reliable option on Results Day. Websites can be slow, wrong, or overloaded with traffic - a real person on the clearing hotline can tell you exactly what's available right now.

Is any of this guaranteed?

No, and we don't want to pretend otherwise. This is a free, independent tool built to help you find a good shortlist of universities and courses quickly - not an official UCAS service, and not connected to UCAS's live data. Course availability, phone lines, and offers can all change without us knowing. Always double-check directly with the university before making any decisions.

What information do you keep about me?

Only what's needed to show you results and let you export or share them - your search (grades, subject, filters) is stored for 30 minutes so the "export" and "share this search" links work, then it's deleted automatically. We don't ask for your name, email, or any account details to use the search tool itself. We do log some technical details (like a rough approximate area from your IP address, and which browser/device type you're using) to understand how the site is used and to keep it running smoothly - not to identify you personally.

Something's wrong or confusing - who do I tell?
